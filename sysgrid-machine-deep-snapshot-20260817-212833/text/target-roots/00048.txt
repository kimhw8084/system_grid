# SysGrid Product and Golden Contract v1.2

**Status:** Draft pending explicit activation  
**Project:** SysGrid  
**Proposed effective date:** 2026-07-30

## 1. Scope gate

Only the Linear project `SysGrid` may be read or written unless the user explicitly names another project and duration. Before a state mutation or artifact issuance, verify live project `SysGrid`, master `OUT-5`, sole active issue, status, relations, latest relevant verdict, accepted baseline, actual repository state, qualified engine, and safe next action.

Only one SysGrid mutation issue may be active. Parent goals provide context, not a parallel mutation lane.

## 2. Source-authoring boundary

The ChatGPT control room authors all SysGrid source changes and immutable patches. Terminal generative coding, coding agents, dynamic patch generation, auto-fix, assertion weakening, manual product edits, and manual product commit/push are prohibited.

## 3. Monitoring golden contract

Monitoring is the immutable high-level visual and behavioral reference unless the user explicitly approves a golden-contract change.

Goldenization means dynamic synchronization of all applicable shared:

- shell, header, command bar, controls, visuals and responsive behavior;
- table/grid framing, columns, rows, cells, selection, grouping, density and sizing;
- loading, empty, error, offline, conflict and permission states;
- overlays, panels, modals, lifecycle actions, receipts and undo;
- routed behavior and convenience features.

A change to an authoritative shared contract propagates to every registered applicable view. Domain views may provide only typed declarative adapters, capability declarations, endpoint/data mappings, explicit slots, and documented N/A exceptions. Local copies of shared behavior or presentation cannot PASS.

Source-file protection alone does not prove rendered golden preservation when a changed shared dependency affects Monitoring. Applicable shared visual changes require routed before/after geometry or screenshot evidence across the relevant states.

## 4. Product architecture boundaries

Unless explicitly authorized:

- do not flatten Network, FAR, or Research into table-only experiences;
- do not modify Projects or Architecture product code outside their approved stage;
- do not replace domain APIs with a giant generic CRUD engine;
- do not replace AG Grid or redesign the complete visual system;
- do not broadly rewrite deployment, authentication, tenant selection, or container infrastructure;
- do not weaken tenant isolation, permissions, assertions, recovery, or destructive-operation safeguards;
- do not mix product and execution-platform changes.

## 5. Material-decision packet

A five-question user approval packet is required only for new behavior, shared-contract changes, data/API semantics, permissions, destructive behavior, migrations, scope expansion, or ambiguous intent:

1. What exact user-visible outcome changes?
2. What is the smallest complete vertical slice?
3. Which shared contract owns it and which views are applicable?
4. What must remain unchanged, including tenant/safety invariants?
5. What exact evidence constitutes acceptance?

Provide principal-engineer recommended answers. The user may approve all or edit numbered rows.

No new approval is required for exact correction of a proven defect, test-only hardening, evidence/capsule-envelope repair with unchanged product bytes, or continuation of an already approved slice.

## 6. Risk-tiered proof

Risk tier changes early feedback order, not the final acceptance bar.

### Tier 0 — metadata/docs/declarative maps

Schema, syntax, identity, and relevant contract tests.

### Tier 1 — bounded nonvisual implementation

Focused unit/contract tests, architecture checks, typecheck, then the full final app gate.

### Tier 2 — shared behavior or visual contract

Focused integration plus all affected routed views/states, responsive geometry or screenshots, then the full final app gate.

### Tier 3 — tenant, permission, data model, migration, or destructive behavior

Negative authorization/isolation tests, migration/recovery tests, off-nominal routed proof, then the full final app gate.

### Tier 4 — execution platform

Released-byte fault injection and independent platform qualification; no product mutation.

## 7. Product proof order

Use economic fail-fast order:

1. capsule/schema/safety;
2. live issue and repository baseline;
3. patch dry-apply and exact state transition;
4. cheapest focused architecture/static tests;
5. focused unit/contract/integration tests;
6. typecheck and build;
7. runtime identity, routes, tenant/profile and fixtures;
8. critical routed workflow and relevant off-nominal states;
9. bounded adjacent regressions;
10. full `verify-app.sh` or qualified equivalent;
11. exact final/protected hashes;
12. commit, push, remote equality and clean state;
13. canonical packaging.

## 8. Commit and push

A successful bounded product mutation creates one commit:

```text
OUT-<issue> <stage> Iteration <N>: <accepted slice title>
```

The engine verifies upstream identity, pushes the exact commit, reads the remote branch SHA, requires local/remote equality, and requires clean worktree and staging before packaging success.

Qualification, read-only, acquisition, and failed runs never commit or push.

## 9. Recovery

Use the narrowest correction matching evidence:

- product defect → new cumulative product patch;
- exact retained post-state → `resume_verify`, never reapply;
- stale test/fixture → bounded test-contract correction;
- environment drift → resolve environment without source mutation;
- executor defect → platform lane only, product bytes frozen.
